# Visual system

Experience mode. The full-viewport bowling alley leads; the app has no brand name, wordmark or venue slogan.

The supplied gameplay video establishes the presentation. The user's camera correction takes priority: preserve the original aiming angle at release, then dolly smoothly along the lane to follow the ball and hold on pin impact. Never cut angles during an active delivery. Only after the physical delivery resolves, show a slow-motion replay and character reaction. Replays use recorded transforms from the actual shot and never resimulate or rescore it. Reduced motion keeps a fixed delivery camera and omits replay and reaction cuts.

Materials move toward realism while retaining readable forms: lacquered maple grain, charcoal gutters, brushed-metal rails, sea-green pinsetter panels, porcelain pins with red neck bands, marbled blue resin balls, soft ceiling lighting and lane reflections. The original articulated character has sculpted hair, facial features, a teal collared shirt, navy trousers and bowling shoes.

The interface uses Trebuchet MS, ink #203d4a, cool white #f7faf8 and blue #216e88. A compact setup sheet sits left; settings sit top right. The score strip stays low. Camera tracking recedes during a delivery and returns for the next swing. A plain replay label identifies the recorded view. Controls retain visible focus, legible labels and mobile layouts.

The independent body-tracking page is an operating surface: a full-height camera image with mirrored segmentation outline, full-body joint connections, uncertainty feedback, orientation controls and a full-screen button. Video and overlays share an aspect ratio. Green means tracked; dashed amber means uncertain. Stale outlines disappear. Processing remains local.

The desktop camera inset grows to 420 px, constrained on shorter screens. Aim controls sit above the scorecard. A deliberately raised non-bowling hand engages motion aiming; torso-relative sideways travel adjusts the direction, lowering locks it, and swing recognition rearms afterward. Slider, buttons and keyboard remain available. The current controlling hand is highlighted in the inset. Optional shot variation is sampled once at release and kept small enough for deliberate aim to dominate.

The bowler has elbow and knee articulation, a planted lead foot and eased recovery. Camera clearance fades only the character, never the delivered ball. Four seated background spectators have varied clothing and skin/hair colors, restrained idle motion and celebration responses.
